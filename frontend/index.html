The engineering problem and our answer

Particle size decides bed permeability, extraction kinetics and, in the end, flavour.

Conventional open-loop grinding

Burr and disc grinders break beans in a single pass and ship whatever comes out, which gives a broad, bimodal distribution:

  • Boulders. Low surface area and long diffusion paths; water bypasses them, so they under-extract and taste sour.
  • Fines (< 100 µm). They dominate the bed's surface area, raise pressure drop (Ergun equation), migrate into filter pores and over-extract into bitterness.
  • Heat. High-speed friction warms the grounds and drives off volatile aroma compounds.
  • No correction. Without classification, off-size material goes straight into the product.

The BrewDz closed loop

BrewDz treats grinding as a classification problem, with every stream accounted for:

  • Chilled low-shear roller mill. Water-jacketed rolls with a set gap give a narrow main mode and fewer fracture fines.
  • Vibrating screen. A defined aperture removes everything coarser than the target cut.
  • Oversize recycle. Screen oversize returns to the mill instead of leaving as waste.
  • Cyclone de-dusting. Super-fines are pulled out as a separate by-product stream for soluble-coffee extraction.

Aspen Plus V14 Flowsheet Simulation & Rigorous Solids Balances

Charan's Aspen Plus V14 model (Coffee_powder.apwz) simulating the continuous Roaster-Mixer-Crusher-Screen circuit with steady-state recycle convergence.

Aspen Circuit Architecture

The solids flowsheet was built in Aspen Plus V14 using the MIXNCPSD stream class to rigorously model non-conventional coffee solids across 30 particle size mesh intervals:

  • Feed Conditioning (FEED & ROASTER): $100\,\text{kg/h}$ total feed ($97\,\text{kg/h}$ coffee solids + $3\,\text{kg/h}$ moisture) enters at $25^\circ\text{C}$ and is roasted up to $210^\circ\text{C}$ at $1.013\,\text{bar}$, flashing moisture into vapor.
  • Solids Mixing (MIXER): Fresh roasted coffee merges with recycled screen oversize ($22.5\text{--}23.2\,\text{kg/h}$), loading the crusher at $\approx 120.0\,\text{kg/h}$.
  • Mechanical Comminution (CRUSHER): Double roll crushing fractures beans to $d_p < 2087\,\mu\text{m}$.
  • Multi-Deck Classification (SCREEN): Generates three calibrated streams:
    • PROD: $41.5\text{--}42.0\,\text{kg/h}$ on-spec product ($67.4\%$ in $600\text{--}1100\,\mu\text{m}$; $0.00\%$ boulders $>1590\,\mu\text{m}$).
    • FINE: $55.0\text{--}55.5\,\text{kg/h}$ dust ($100\% < 597\,\mu\text{m}$) for instant coffee soluble extraction.
    • OVERSIZE: $22.5\text{--}23.2\,\text{kg/h}$ coarse recycle returned to the mixer ($R/F \approx 23.5\%$).

Method and verification

The governing equations, a live hand-check at your operating point, and what the model does not cover.

Governing equations

Mill product (per pass)
Q(d) = φf · LN(d; 40 µm, σg 1.8) + (1 − φf) · [1 − exp(−(d / d63)n)]
Double roll angle of nip & gripping check
cos(α/2) = (DR + s) / (DR + Dp),   μ ≥ tan(α/2) ⇒ α ≤ 35.5°
Double roll practical capacity
Qpractical = 60 π DR WR N s ρbulk ηv
Recycle balance
M = F + R,   R = r · M  ⇒  M = F / (1 − r)
Screen effectiveness
E = EO · EU = (O xO / F xF) · (U (1 − xU) / F (1 − xF))
Bond's law (closed circuit, fresh feed basis)
P = ṁF · 10 Wi (1/√P80 − 1/√F80)
Ergun equation (brew bed)
ΔP / L = 150 μu (1 − ε)2 / (ε3 d322) + 1.75 ρu2 (1 − ε) / (ε3 d32)

Simulation & Modeling Notes

  • Aspen Plus V14 Flowsheet Benchmark. The circuit mass balance and particle classification are validated against Charan's Aspen Plus V14 solids simulation (Coffee_powder.apwz, 12 runs: mix_1..6 vs. run_1..6). Closing the oversize recycle loop delivers a +23.8% surge in product yield (42.8% vs. 34.6%).
  • Material assumptions. Bond work index $W_i = 12.5\,\text{kWh/t}$ and moisture fraction ($3\%$) reflect typical roasted coffee bean physical characteristics.
  • Recycle breakage. Recycled oversize is modeled as re-breaking to the calibrated product PSD shape without accumulation.
  • Conventional baseline. The open-loop grinder baseline is modeled as a broad bimodal PSD representative of commercial batch disc/burr grinders.
  • Pressure drop is relative. Ergun equation evaluates clean-bed hydraulic permeability. Real puck tamping and swelling are not dynamically simulated.
  • Chilled grinding. The < 28 °C roll jacket temperature is maintained to preserve delicate coffee aromatics.
